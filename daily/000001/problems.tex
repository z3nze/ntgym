\begin{enumerate}
\item \[
        \gcd(a, b)=1, \qquad a \mid bc
        \quad \implies \quad
        a \mid c
        \]
        Prove it from Bézout's identity rather than prime factorization.
\item Prove that there are infinitely many primes
        \[
        p \equiv 3 \mod{4}
        \]
        A Euclid-style argument works, but simply taking the product of known \(3\bmod4\) primes and adding \(1\) does not give the useful construction directly. Try to determine what number to construct and why at least one of its prime divisors must be \(3\bmod4\).
\end{enumerate}
